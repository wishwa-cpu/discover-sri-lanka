About Sri Lanka

The Resplendent Isle

Sri Lanka — formerly Ceylon — is a teardrop-shaped island nation nestled in the Indian Ocean, just off the southern tip of India. With a landmass of 65,610 km², it packs extraordinary diversity into a compact space.

Home to eight UNESCO World Heritage Sites, 26 national parks, ancient Buddhist monuments, colonial-era forts, lush tea plantations, and over 1,500 km of coastline, Sri Lanka is one of the world's most biodiverse and culturally rich destinations.

Two major ethnic groups — Sinhalese and Tamil — along with Moors, Burghers, and other communities, have shaped a vibrant multicultural nation with three official languages: Sinhala, Tamil, and English.

Through the Ages

2,500 Years of Civilization

From the legendary arrival of Prince Vijaya to modern independence, Sri Lanka's history is as rich as its landscape.

543 BC
Founding of the Kingdom
Prince Vijaya arrives from India, founding the first Sinhalese kingdom.
247 BC
Buddhism Arrives
Mahinda, son of Emperor Ashoka, brings Buddhism to Sri Lanka during the reign of King Devanampiyatissa.
161 BC
King Dutugamunu Unifies the Island
King Dutugamunu defeats Elara and unifies the island under one kingdom, building the Ruwanwelisaya stupa.
1017 AD
Chola Invasion
South Indian Chola dynasty conquers the island; Polonnaruwa becomes the new capital.
1505
Portuguese Arrival
Portuguese sailors establish the first European presence, beginning the colonial era in coastal Sri Lanka.
1658
Dutch Take Control
The Dutch East India Company ousts the Portuguese and controls coastal areas and the cinnamon trade.
1815
British Crown Colony
The British conquer the Kandyan Kingdom — the last independent kingdom — and Ceylon becomes a British Crown Colony.
1948
Independence
Ceylon gains independence from Britain on February 4th, 1948 — now celebrated as National Independence Day.
1972
Republic of Sri Lanka
Ceylon is renamed the Republic of Sri Lanka, becoming a republic with a new constitution.
2009
End of Civil War
After 26 years, the civil conflict ends. Sri Lanka begins a new chapter of peace and reconstruction.
Present
Modern Renaissance
Sri Lanka emerges as a top travel destination, with growing tech, tourism, and cultural industries.

Living Traditions

Culture & Heritage

Two thousand years of art, faith, dance, and festivity woven into everyday Sri Lankan life.

Kandyan Dancing

One of Sri Lanka's most treasured art forms, Kandyan dance is an elaborate performance of colorful costumes, rhythmic drumming, and acrobatic movements — traditionally performed at temples and royal courts. The dancers' elaborate headdresses and costumes are iconic symbols of Kandyan culture.

Buddhist Heritage

Buddhism has shaped Sri Lankan civilization for over 2,300 years. From the massive dagobas of Anuradhapura to the serene rock temples of Dambulla, Buddhist art and architecture define the island's spiritual landscape. Poya (full moon) days are national holidays honoring the lunar calendar.

Tamil Heritage

Sri Lankan Tamil culture is vibrant and ancient, expressed through classical Bharatanatyam dance, Carnatic music, intricate kolam floor patterns, magnificent Hindu kovils, and the rich literary tradition in the Tamil language — one of the world's oldest classical languages.

Festivals & Celebrations

🎊

Sinhala & Tamil New Year

Avurudu — celebrated on April 13/14, marking the traditional new year with rituals, games, sweets, and family gatherings.

🪔

Deepavali

The Hindu festival of lights — celebrated by Tamil communities with oil lamps, fireworks, and sweets in October/November.

🌕

Vesak

The most sacred Buddhist festival celebrating the birth, enlightenment, and passing of the Buddha — streets lit with lanterns in May.

🌾

Thai Pongal

Tamil harvest festival in January, giving thanks to the sun, cattle, and nature with the cooking of the sacred pongal rice.

🐘

Esala Perahera

Kandy's grand procession — ten days of illuminated elephants, fire dancers, and drummers parading through the city in July/August.

☪️

Eid Al-Fitr

Celebrated by Sri Lankan Muslims marking the end of Ramadan with prayers, feasting, and giving to the poor.

A Feast for the Senses

Sri Lankan Cuisine

Bold spices, tropical flavors, and centuries of culinary tradition — Sri Lankan food is a true adventure.

Rice and Curry

The national dish — steamed rice served with a spread of curries, sambol, papadums, and pickles. Every household has its own version, using local spices like goraka, pandan, and curry leaves.

🇱🇰 Island-wide staple

Hoppers (Appa)

Bowl-shaped pancakes made from fermented rice flour and coconut milk, served plain, with egg, or sweet. A beloved breakfast staple across the island, typically paired with pol sambol.

🌿 All regions

Kottu Roti

Sri Lanka's beloved street food — shredded flatbread stir-fried with vegetables, egg, and meat on a sizzling griddle. The metallic clanging of the blades chopping the roti is the sound of Sri Lankan street life.

🌆 Urban street food

String Hoppers

Delicate steamed noodle patties made from rice flour pressed through a mould into fine strands. Served with coconut milk, dhal curry, and seeni sambol — a light, satisfying breakfast or dinner.

🏠 Home comfort food

Lamprais

A Dutch-Burgher legacy — rice cooked in stock with Dutch-style curries, a meatball, and sambol, all wrapped in a banana leaf and baked. A Sunday feast tradition in Sri Lanka's Burgher community.

🇳🇱 Burgher heritage

Seafood

Surrounded by the Indian Ocean, Sri Lanka offers spectacular seafood — devilled prawns, black pepper crab, ambul thiyal (sour fish curry), and fresh tuna, often cooked with tangy goraka and coconut milk.

🌊 Coastal specialty

Pol Roti

Thick, unleavened flatbread made with freshly grated coconut and wheat flour, cooked on a griddle. Served with lunu miris (onion chili sambol) and dhal — a staple breakfast in coastal areas.

🥥 Coastal favorite

Sweets & Desserts

Kiri bath (milk rice), watalappan (Malay coconut custard), kavum (oil cakes), kokis (crispy Dutch cookies), and buffalo curd with kithul treacle — Sri Lanka's desserts are a sweet celebration of its diverse heritage.

🍮 Festive tradition
Wild Sri Lanka

Wildlife & Nature

One of the world's biodiversity hotspots — 26 national parks protecting extraordinary wildlife and ancient forests.

🐘 Sri Lankan Elephant

The largest land animal in Asia, with over 7,000 wild elephants — one of the highest densities in Asia. The Elephant Gathering at Minneriya is one of the greatest wildlife spectacles on Earth.

⚠️ Vulnerable

🐆 Sri Lankan Leopard

Found nowhere else — the Sri Lankan leopard is the apex predator of the island's forests. Yala National Park holds the world's highest density of wild leopards, making sightings relatively common.

🔴 Endangered

🐋 Blue Whales & Dolphins

Off the southern coast near Mirissa and Trincomalee, blue whales — the largest animals on Earth — can be spotted year-round. Spinner dolphin pods of hundreds are a common sight near the coast.

🔴 Endangered

🦜 Bird Watching

Over 1,340 species including 27 endemic birds such as the Sri Lanka junglefowl, Sri Lanka blue magpie, and Sri Lanka drongo. Sinharaja Forest Reserve is a world-class birding destination.

✅ Protected

🐢 Sea Turtles

Five of the world's seven sea turtle species nest on Sri Lanka's beaches. Kosgoda and Rekawa are famous for turtle watching at night as leatherback, hawksbill, and green turtles lay their eggs.

🔴 Protected

🌿 Sinharaja Rainforest

Sri Lanka's last viable remnant of primary tropical rainforest — a UNESCO World Heritage site with over 95% endemic trees, rare amphibians, and extraordinary biodiversity in just 89 km².

✅ UNESCO Protected

Getting Around

Transport Guide

Sri Lanka is compact and well-connected — here's how to explore it like a local.

🚂

Scenic Trains

Sri Lanka's railways are legendary. The Kandy–Ella route through tea country is consistently rated one of the world's most beautiful train journeys. Book 2nd class observation cars or sit in open doors for the full experience.

💡 Book Expo Rail seats in advance
🚌

Buses

The cheapest way to get around. SLTB government buses cover every corner of the island, while private intercity buses are faster. Air-conditioned express coaches link major cities comfortably.

💡 AC buses: 3–4x the normal price
🛺

Tuk-Tuks

The iconic three-wheeled tuk-tuk (auto rickshaw) is the quintessential Sri Lankan experience. Perfect for short trips and exploring towns. Always negotiate the price before getting in — or use PickMe app.

💡 Use PickMe for metered rides
✈️

Domestic Flights

Cinnamon Air and FitsAir operate seaplane and light aircraft services connecting Colombo to Jaffna, Trincomalee, Batticaloa, and Ratmalana. Fast but pricey — ideal for saving time.

💡 Seaplanes land on lagoons
🚗

Car Hire & Drivers

Hiring a car with a driver for a week is one of the best ways to explore freely. Rates are reasonable and drivers double as guides and local experts. International driving permits allow self-driving.

💡 LKD required for self-drive
🛥️

Ferries & Boats

Boat rides are essential for reaching Nainativu Island near Jaffna, whale watching at Mirissa, Madu River mangrove cruises, and crossing lagoons to reach remote beaches along the east coast.

💡 Whale watching: Nov–Apr
